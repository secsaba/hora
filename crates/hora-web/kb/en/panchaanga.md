# Panchaanga

The panchaanga is a moment described by five measures of time, its five limbs: pancha means five and anga a limb. The word also names the almanac that lists those limbs day by day. Each limb has its own page here, and this one puts them together.

## The five limbs

- **[Tithi](/en/kb/tithi)** (lunar day): which twelve-degree step of the Moon's gain on the Sun is running.
- **[Vaara](/en/kb/vaara-and-hora)** (weekday): the weekday of the sunrise that opened the Vedic day.
- **[Nakshatra](/en/kb/nakshatra)** (constellation): which of the 27 nakshatras the Moon is in.
- **[Sun-Moon yoga](/en/kb/sun-moon-yoga)**: which of 27 equal parts the sum of the Sun's and the Moon's longitudes falls in.
- **[Karana](/en/kb/karana)**: which half of the tithi is running.

Four of the five come from the Sun and the Moon alone, so at a given instant they are the same everywhere on Earth. Only the vaara depends on the place, since it is counted from the local sunrise. The ayanamsa touches them unevenly too: the tithi and the karana measure the Moon against the Sun, so the [ayanamsa](/en/kb/ayanamsa) drops out of them, while the nakshatra and the Sun-Moon yoga shift with it.

## An almanac

In India the yearly almanacs that print the [grahas'](/en/kb/graha) (planets') positions are called panchaangas too. Besides the positions, they give for every day the limbs that run on it and the times each one starts and ends, since a tithi, a nakshatra, a Sun-Moon yoga or a karana can change at any hour. The chart page does the same for a single moment.

## Muhurta

A muhurta (electional astrology) is a time picked for starting something: a journey, a building, a marriage. The panchaanga is where the choice begins: a good muhurta has a favourable tithi, vaara, nakshatra, Sun-Moon yoga and karana all at once. Which limbs are favourable for what is a subject of its own, and hora does not pick muhurtas.

## A worked example

On 1 January 2000 at 12:00 UT, at Greenwich ([see the chart](/en/chart?date=2000-01-01&time=12%3A00&calendar=gregorian&utc=stated&offset=%2B00%3A00&latitude=51.48&longitude=0&altitude=&ayanamsa=lahiri&node=mean&sunrise=upper-limb-apparent&reference=lagna&style=south)), hora gives these five limbs:

| Limb | Value | Lord | Started | Ends |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tithi | Krishna Ekadasi (26) | Mars | 05:34:43 | 07:50:31 on 2 January |
| Vaara | Saturday | Saturn | 08:05:47 | 08:05:41 on 2 January |
| Nakshatra | Swaati (15) | Rahu | 10:37:39 on 31 December 1999 | 13:03:25 |
| Sun-Moon yoga | Dhriti (8) | | 07:06:45 | 07:43:37 on 2 January |
| Karana | Bava | | 05:34:43 | 18:40:01 |

The tithi and its karana started together, since Bava is the first half of Krishna Ekadasi. The vaara runs from sunrise to sunrise. The nakshatra is about to end: an hour after noon the Moon leaves Swaati, while the other limbs last into the evening or the next morning.
