# Kinds of bhavas

The twelve [bhavas](/en/kb/bhava) (houses) fall into groups by their number, and each group does the same kind of work for whatever it is counted from. The groups are counted from any reference point: the lagna, the Moon, a special lagna, paaka lagna ([what each shows](/en/kb/bhava-significations)). They can also be counted from a bhava: the nth bhava from bhava b is b + n − 1, less 12 past the 12th.

## Kendra {#kendra}

The kendras (quadrants) are the 1st, 4th, 7th and 10th bhavas. They hold up what they are counted from and keep it going. Counted from the lagna they are the body, the home, marriage and work, the four pillars a life stands on. Two grahas in kendras from each other, such as one in the 4th from the other, support each other.

## Trikona {#trikona}

The trikonas (trines) are the 1st, 5th and 9th bhavas. They make what they are counted from flourish and prosper: the trikonas from the lagna show how well the person fares. Two grahas in trikonas from each other help each other thrive.

## Panaphara {#panaphara}

The panapharas (succedants) are the 2nd, 5th, 8th and 11th bhavas: the kendras counted from the 2nd. The book names them in chapter 7 and puts them to work later, with [dasas](/en/kb/dasa) (periods) counted over kendras, panapharas and apoklimas in turn.

## Apoklima {#apoklima}

The apoklimas (precedants) are the 3rd, 6th, 9th and 12th bhavas: the kendras counted from the 3rd. With the kendras and panapharas they share out all twelve bhavas, four each.

## Upachaya {#upachaya}

The upachayas are the 3rd, 6th, 10th and 11th bhavas. They bring growth and gain to what they are counted from: the upachayas from the hora lagna, for instance, show wealth increasing.

## Dusthana {#dusthana}

The dusthanas (bad houses) are the 6th, 8th and 12th bhavas. They bring setbacks to what they are counted from. The stronger a dusthana, the harder its obstacles: one made strong, or occupied by malefic grahas, can bring serious trouble, while a weak one brings troubles that are easily got over.

## Chaturasra {#chaturasra}

The chaturasras are the 4th and 8th bhavas. Chapter 7 only names them; later chapters use them.

## The four purushaartha trikonas {#purushaartha}

A purushaartha (aim of life) is one of the four things a human life pursues: dharma (righteous duty), artha (wealth), kaama (desire) and moksha (liberation). Each has its trikona, the trikonas counted from one of the first four bhavas:

- **Dharma trikona**, from the 1st: the 1st, 5th and 9th. The self, its intelligence and its sense of right show how one keeps to one's duty.
- **Artha trikona**, from the 2nd: the 2nd, 6th and 10th. Wealth, service and career show how one earns and manages one's livelihood.
- **Kaama trikona**, from the 3rd: the 3rd, 7th and 11th. Effort, relationships and gains show what one desires and how one gets it.
- **Moksha trikona**, from the 4th: the 4th, 8th and 12th. Inner peace, the search for hidden truth and letting go show the path to liberation.

How strongly the grahas stand in each of these trikonas shows how much each aim weighs in a life. The book returns to them with its digbala (directional strength) and with Trikona dasa.

## The visible and invisible halves {#visible-half}

Counted from any reference point, the 7th to 12th bhavas are the visible half: what they bring shows in the outer world, where others can see it. The 1st to 6th are the invisible half, and what they bring is harder to see from outside.

## In short

| Kind | Bhavas | What it brings |
|---|---|---|
| Kendra (quadrant) | 1, 4, 7, 10 | Support and lasting activity |
| Trikona (trine) | 1, 5, 9 | Prosperity |
| Panaphara (succedant) | 2, 5, 8, 11 | Named now, used later |
| Apoklima (precedant) | 3, 6, 9, 12 | Named now, used later |
| Upachaya | 3, 6, 10, 11 | Growth and gains |
| Dusthana (bad house) | 6, 8, 12 | Setbacks and obstacles |
| Chaturasra | 4, 8 | Named now, used later |
| Visible half | 7 to 12 | Results the world can see |
| Invisible half | 1 to 6 | Results that stay hidden |

## A worked example

Take hora's chart for 1 January 2000 at 12:00 UT, at Greenwich ([see the chart](/en/chart?date=2000-01-01&time=12%3A00&calendar=gregorian&utc=stated&offset=%2B00%3A00&latitude=51.48&longitude=0&altitude=&ayanamsa=lahiri&node=mean&sunrise=upper-limb-apparent&reference=lagna&style=south)), counted from its lagna in Aries. The Bhava column puts Jupiter and Saturn in the 1st, Rahu in the 4th, the Moon in the 7th, Venus in the 8th, the Sun and Mercury in the 9th, Ketu in the 10th and Mars in the 11th.

1. Kendras: every one is occupied, by Jupiter and Saturn (1st), Rahu (4th), the Moon (7th) and Ketu (10th).
2. Trikonas: Jupiter and Saturn (1st) and the Sun and Mercury (9th); the 5th is empty. The Sun is in a trikona from Jupiter, too: the 9th from him.
3. Upachayas: Ketu (10th) and Mars (11th).
4. Dusthanas: Venus alone, in the 8th.
5. Visible half: the Moon, Venus, the Sun, Mercury, Ketu and Mars. Invisible half: Jupiter, Saturn and Rahu.
6. From a bhava: the kaama trikona, the trikonas from the 3rd, is the 3rd, 7th and 11th, and holds the Moon and Mars.

The same groups counted from another reference point fall on other grahas. From paaka lagna, the rasi of the lagna's lord, here Aquarius ([see the chart](/en/chart?date=2000-01-01&time=12%3A00&calendar=gregorian&utc=stated&offset=%2B00%3A00&latitude=51.48&longitude=0&altitude=&ayanamsa=lahiri&node=mean&sunrise=upper-limb-apparent&reference=paaka-lagna&style=south)), the kendras hold only Mars (1st) and Venus (10th), and the dusthanas hold Rahu (6th) and Ketu (12th).

## In the chart

The chart page does not mark these groups; the Bhava columns give each graha's bhava from the chosen reference point, and the groups can be read off them.
