# The twenty varga chakras

A [varga chakra](/en/kb/varga-chakra) (divisional chart) cuts every [rasi](/en/kb/rasi) (sign) into parts and gives each part a rasi of its own. The book teaches twenty of them in chapter 6, from the rasi chart, which does not cut the rasi at all, to the shashtyamsa, which cuts it into sixty. This page gives each one's rule and the area of life it is read for.

## How a varga places a body

To place a body in a varga chakra, take the part of its rasi it falls in, then the rasi that part is given. That rasi is the body's place in the varga chakra, and nothing more: a varga chakra gives no degrees. The same goes for every body of the chart: each [graha](/en/kb/graha) (planet), the lagna (ascendant), the [upagrahas](/en/kb/upagraha) (sub-planets) and the [special lagnas](/en/kb/special-lagna) (special ascendants).

Most rules count rasis from a starting rasi: the starting rasi is the 1st, the next one in the zodiac's order the 2nd, and so on. After the twelfth the count goes round again, so the 13th rasi from a rasi is that rasi itself. Where a rule depends on the rasi being divided, it may ask whether that rasi is odd or even, whether it is movable (chara), fixed (sthira) or dual (dwiswabhava), or which element it has; the [rasi characteristics](/en/kb/rasi-characteristics) page lists these.

Each part includes its first point but not its last: a body at exactly 10° of a rasi is in the second of three parts, not the first. hora decides this to the whole arcsecond.

## Rasi chart (D-1) {#d-1}

One part, the whole rasi, given to the rasi itself: the rasi chart is the varga chakra that divides nothing. It shows life at the level of the physical body and its existence in the world.

## Hora (D-2) {#d-2}

Two halves of 15°. In an odd rasi the first half is the Sun's hora and the second the Moon's; in an even rasi it is the other way round. The book names the halves only by the Sun and the Moon; hora puts the Sun's hora in Leo and the Moon's in Cancer, the rasis they own, as the book's own amsabala example requires. It shows wealth and money. The book notes that using the hora chart properly needs more than it teaches, and sets it aside; hora still draws it, and it still counts in [amsabala](/en/kb/amsabala).

## Drekkana (D-3) {#d-3}

Three parts of 10°: the first goes to the rasi itself, the second to the 5th rasi from it, the third to the 9th. It shows brothers and sisters and all that concerns them.

## Chaturthamsa (D-4) {#d-4}

Four parts of 7°30′, given to the 1st, 4th, 7th and 10th rasis from the rasi. It shows one's home and the houses and property one owns, and fortune.

## Panchamsa (D-5) {#d-5}

Five parts of 6°. In an odd rasi they go, in order, to Aries, Aquarius, Sagittarius, Gemini and Libra; in an even rasi to Taurus, Virgo, Pisces, Capricorn and Scorpio. It shows fame, power and authority.

## Shashthamsa (D-6) {#d-6}

Six parts of 5°, counted from Aries in an odd rasi and from Libra in an even one. It shows illness and troubles of health.

## Saptamsa (D-7) {#d-7}

Seven parts of about 4°17′09″, counted from the rasi itself in an odd rasi and from the 7th rasi from it in an even one. It shows children and grandchildren.

## Ashtamsa (D-8) {#d-8}

Eight parts of 3°45′, counted from Aries in a movable rasi, from Sagittarius in a fixed one and from Leo in a dual one. It shows sudden, unforeseen trouble, lawsuits among them.

## Navamsa (D-9) {#d-9}

Nine parts of 3°20′, counted from Aries in a fiery rasi, from Capricorn in an earthy one, from Libra in an airy one and from Cancer in a watery one. It is the most used varga chakra after the rasi chart, and shows a great deal: marriage and the spouse, dharma (duty and right conduct), how one deals with other people, one's basic skills and the inner self.

## Dasamsa (D-10) {#d-10}

Ten parts of 3°, counted from the rasi itself in an odd rasi and from the 9th rasi from it in an even one. It shows career: what one does and achieves in society.

## Rudramsa (D-11) {#d-11}

Eleven parts of about 2°43′38″. Count how far the rasi stands from Aries going forward through the zodiac, then count the same number of rasis from Aries going backward; the parts are counted forward from the rasi reached. Libra, the 7th from Aries, starts from Libra again, since the 7th backward from Aries is Libra too; Taurus, the 2nd, starts from Pisces. It shows death and destruction.

## Dwadasamsa (D-12) {#d-12}

Twelve parts of 2°30′, counted from the rasi itself. It shows the parents and their blood relatives: uncles, aunts and grandparents.

## Shodasamsa (D-16) {#d-16}

Sixteen parts of 1°52′30″, counted from Aries in a movable rasi, from Leo in a fixed one and from Sagittarius in a dual one. It shows vehicles, comforts and pleasures, and their absence.

## Vimsamsa (D-20) {#d-20}

Twenty parts of 1°30′, counted from Aries in a movable rasi, from Sagittarius in a fixed one and from Leo in a dual one: the ashtamsa's starting rasis, not the shodasamsa's. It shows religious and spiritual life.

## Chaturvimsamsa (D-24) {#d-24}

Twenty-four parts of 1°15′, counted from Leo in an odd rasi and from Cancer in an even one. It shows learning, knowledge and education.

## Nakshatramsa (D-27) {#d-27}

Twenty-seven parts of 1°06′40″, counted from Aries in a fiery rasi, from Cancer in an earthy one, from Libra in an airy one and from Capricorn in a watery one: the earthy and watery starts are the navamsa's swapped. It shows one's strengths and weaknesses and inborn nature.

## Trimsamsa (D-30) {#d-30}

The one varga chakra with unequal parts: five of them, marked off at whole degrees. In an odd rasi they span 5°, 5°, 8°, 7° and 5°, and go to Aries, Aquarius, Sagittarius, Gemini and Libra; in an even rasi they span 5°, 7°, 8°, 5° and 5°, and go to Taurus, Virgo, Pisces, Capricorn and Scorpio. It shows misfortune and punishment, the subconscious self, and some illnesses.

## Khavedamsa (D-40) {#d-40}

Forty parts of 45′, counted from Aries in an odd rasi and from Libra in an even one. It shows auspicious and inauspicious events.

## Akshavedamsa (D-45) {#d-45}

Forty-five parts of 40′, counted from Aries in a movable rasi, from Leo in a fixed one and from Sagittarius in a dual one. It is read for every matter.

## Shashtyamsa (D-60) {#d-60}

Sixty parts of 30′, counted from the rasi itself. It shows the karma carried over from past lives, and is read for every matter too.

## Four planes

The number of parts says on which plane of existence a varga chakra works. Those of up to twelve parts, D-1 to D-12, show the physical plane: the body, wealth, home, spouse, children and parents. D-16, D-20 and D-24 show the mental plane: pleasure and sorrow, faith, learning. D-27 and D-30 show the subconscious: one's nature, strengths and weaknesses, and hidden flaws. D-40, D-45 and D-60, the finest, show a plane beyond body, mind and subconscious, shaped by karma from past lives. The book advises leaving these last three until one can study them with a teacher.

## Choosing the varga chakra

A question about one area of life is read in the varga chakra that shows that area: a question about career in the dasamsa (D-10), about children in the saptamsa (D-7), about learning in the chaturvimsamsa (D-24). In it, find the grahas, rasis and [bhavas](/en/kb/bhava) (houses) that stand for the matter, and look for the links between them. Since a varga chakra is a chart of its own, its bhavas are counted from where the lagna falls in it.

## Other versions

Some writers give other rules for D-2, D-3, D-8 and D-11, each showing a different side of life. The book teaches only the versions above and leaves the others aside, and hora does the same.

## A worked example

Take hora's chart for 1 January 2000 at 06:00 UT, at Greenwich ([see the chart](/en/chart?date=2000-01-01&time=06%3A00&calendar=gregorian&utc=stated&offset=%2B00%3A00&latitude=51.48&longitude=0&altitude=&ayanamsa=lahiri&node=mean&sunrise=upper-limb-apparent&reference=lagna&style=south&varga=d9)). Its Moon is at 16°27′38″ Libra, an odd, movable, airy rasi, and its lagna at 17°14′32″ Scorpio.

1. Hora (D-2): 16°27′38″ is past 15°, in the second half; in an odd rasi that is the Moon's hora, so Cancer.
2. Drekkana (D-3): between 10° and 20°, the second part, so the 5th rasi from Libra: Aquarius.
3. Navamsa (D-9): each part is 3°20′, and 16°27′38″ lies between 13°20′ and 16°40′, in the 5th part. An airy rasi counts from Libra, and the 5th rasi from Libra is Aquarius.
4. Rudramsa (D-11): Libra is the 7th rasi from Aries, and the 7th counted backward from Aries is Libra again. Each part is about 2°43′38″; the 7th part runs from about 16°21′49″ to 19°05′27″ and holds the Moon. The 7th rasi from Libra is Aries.
5. Trimsamsa (D-30): in an odd rasi the part from 10° to 18° goes to Sagittarius.
6. The lagna in the navamsa: 17°14′32″ lies between 16°40′ and 20°, the 6th part. Scorpio is watery, so the count starts at Cancer, and the 6th rasi from Cancer is Sagittarius.

So in the navamsa the lagna is in Sagittarius and the Moon in Aquarius, the 3rd bhava from it, while in the rasi chart the Moon is in the 12th bhava from the lagna.

## In the chart

The chart page draws one varga chakra beside the rasi chart: the navamsa (D-9), unless another is picked from the row of links D-1 to D-60 above it. It shows the lagna (La), each graha, with R on a retrograde one, and the hora and ghati lagnas, each in its rasi and with no degrees. In the North Indian style its bhavas are counted from the rasi the chosen reference point falls in, in that varga chakra. Under it, the Vargas table gives the rasi of the lagna, the nine grahas and the hora, ghati and sree lagnas in each of the twenty varga chakras. hora places the upagrahas in the varga chakras too, but the chart page does not show them there.

Next comes [amsabala](/en/kb/amsabala): a graha's strength counted over groups of these varga chakras.
