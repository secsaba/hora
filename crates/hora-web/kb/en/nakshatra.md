# Nakshatra

The twelve [rasis](/en/kb/rasi) (signs) are not the only way to cut up the zodiac. A finer and older division splits it into twenty-seven nakshatras (constellations), each named after a group of stars along the Moon's path. The Moon passes through about one nakshatra a day, so they suit the Moon especially well, but every point of a chart, each [graha](/en/kb/graha) (planet) and the lagna (ascendant), stands in one of them.

## Twenty-seven parts of 13°20′

Divide the 360° of the nirayana (sidereal) zodiac by 27 and each part spans 13°20′. The first, Aswini, starts at 0° Aries, the same point where the first rasi starts, and the last, Revati, ends at 360°, where the circle closes. Nine nakshatras fit into every four rasis, so a nakshatra may start in one rasi and end in the next: Punarvasu, the seventh, starts in Gemini and ends in Cancer.

## Four padas

Each nakshatra is again cut into four padas (quarters) of 3°20′. A rasi of 30° holds exactly nine of them, so no pada ever crosses from one rasi into the next. hora numbers the padas 1 to 4 within each nakshatra.

## Lords and deities

Every nakshatra has a Vimsottari lord: one of the nine grahas, taken in a fixed cycle that starts with Ketu at Aswini and runs nine nakshatras long, so it goes round three times in the zodiac. The lords matter most for the Vimsottari dasa, a way of dividing a life into periods ruled by the grahas, which the book teaches in chapter 16; hora does not calculate dasas yet.

Each nakshatra also has a ruling deity, a figure of Vedic lore whose nature colours it.

## Abhijit

Abhijit is a name given to the fourth pada of Uttaraashaadha, from 6°40′ to 10°00′ in Capricorn. Treated as a nakshatra of its own, it makes a count of twenty-eight, but only a few special charts use that count, the Kota and Sarvatobhadra chakras among them. hora, like most of Jyotish, counts twenty-seven.

## A worked example

On 1 January 2000 at 12:00 UT, at Greenwich, hora places the Sun at 16°31′17″ in Sagittarius, a longitude of 256°31′17″ ([see the chart](/en/chart?date=2000-01-01&time=12%3A00&calendar=gregorian&utc=stated&offset=%2B00%3A00&latitude=51.48&longitude=0&altitude=&ayanamsa=lahiri&node=mean&sunrise=upper-limb-apparent&reference=lagna&style=south); the [rasi](/en/kb/rasi) page works out that longitude).

1. 256°31′17″ divided by 13°20′ is 19, with 3°11′17″ left over: nineteen nakshatras are complete, so the Sun is in the twentieth, Poorvaashaadha, 3°11′17″ past its start at 13 Sg 20.
2. 3°11′17″ is less than one pada of 3°20′, so the Sun is in pada 1.
3. Poorvaashaadha's Vimsottari lord, from the table, is Venus.
