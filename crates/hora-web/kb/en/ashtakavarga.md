# Ashtakavarga

Ashtaka means "of eight" and varga "a group". Ashtakavarga (AV) judges a chart from eight reference points at once instead of the lagna alone (§12.1). For the lagna and each of the seven planets it says, from each of the eight references, which houses are good for it. A planet well placed from many references is well placed in the chart. The book uses it to weigh a natal chart, but its main use is reading transits.

## The eight references {#references}

The references are the seven [grahas](/en/kb/graha) from the Sun to Saturn, and the lagna. The same eight are the ones that have an ashtakavarga of their own. Rahu and Ketu are neither: the book's eight tables (Tables 19–26) are defined only for the eight and count only from them, so the nodes give no point to anyone and have no table. hora follows the tables (ADR 0015).

## Rekha and karana {#rekha}

Each table belongs to one planet, or to the lagna. For each of the eight references it marks every house, counted from that reference, as benefic or malefic for the table's planet. A benefic house is a **rekha** (a line, a benefic point), also called a sthana. A malefic house is a **karana**, a word this chapter uses in its own sense: it is not the [karana](/en/kb/karana) of the panchaanga, half of a tithi.

Houses are counted as whole rasis from the rasi the reference is in, the 1st being that rasi itself. If Saturn's table gives a rekha in the 3rd house from Mars, and Mars is in Taurus, Saturn gets a rekha in Cancer.

### The bindu trap {#bindu}

Some authors, especially in South India, use the words the other way round: a benefic house is a bindu (a dot), and a rekha means a malefic house (§12.2 footnote 42, §12.8). It is only a difference in naming, but it turns a reading upside down if missed. The book follows Parasara, and so does hora: a rekha is always a benefic point. Check which convention a book uses before comparing its counts. Even this book, in its chapter on transits, once counts the points of an SAV in "bindus" where it means rekhas.

## Bhinna ashtakavarga (BAV) {#bav}

Bhinna means "separate". A planet's bhinna ashtakavarga (BAV) counts, in each rasi, how many of the eight references give it a rekha there, from 0 to 8 (§12.3). Each planet's BAV has the same total in every chart, as its table fixes how many rekhas it gives out.

A planet's count in a rasi is read in three bands:

- **5 to 8: favourable.** The planet is benefic there from most references.
- **4: neutral.**
- **0 to 3: unfavourable.** The planet is malefic there from most references.

The count in the rasi a planet occupies at birth shows how well it is placed in the chart; the count in a rasi it moves through later shows how it fares in that transit.

### The lagna's BAV {#lagna-bav}

Table 26 defines the lagna's ashtakavarga too, so hora shows it. The book never uses it further, and the bands speak of a planet, so the lagna's BAV gets no band. It is also left out of the SAV, which sums only the seven planets (§12.4).

## Prastaara ashtakavarga (PAV) {#pav}

Prastaara means "spread out". A planet's BAV says how many references give it a rekha in a rasi; its prastaara ashtakavarga (PAV) says which ones (§12.6). It is a grid with a row for each reference and a column for each rasi, and each column adds up to the BAV's count in that rasi.

The count is sometimes not enough. When a transit should help one matter, what counts is whether the planet is benefic from the reference tied to that matter, such as the graha that signifies it or the lord of its house, and only the PAV shows that.

## Samudaaya ashtakavarga (SAV) {#sav}

Samudaaya means "group". The samudaaya ashtakavarga (SAV) adds up the seven planets' BAVs in each rasi (§12.4). The book also calls it Sarva ashtakavarga ("all"); hora uses the one name. Its total is always 337, the sum of the seven planets' fixed totals.

The count in a rasi is read in three bands:

- **30 or more: strong.** The matters of the house falling in that rasi flourish, and planets passing through it in transit give good results.
- **25 to 29: average.** The book writes "25–30", but 30 is already strong, so hora puts the line at 29 (ADR 0015).
- **Under 25: weak.** The matters of that house suffer, and transits through it bring bad results.

## In a varga chakra {#in-a-varga}

Ashtakavarga is not for the rasi chart alone (§12.5). Parasara names, at the start of his work, which [varga chakra](/en/kb/varga-chakra) (divisional chart) shows which area of life, and what he teaches later applies to all of them unless he says otherwise. A varga's ashtakavarga uses the same tables, applied to the places in that varga and counted from its own lagna, and the result is read for that varga's matters: a strong SAV in the dasamsa (D-10) speaks of career, for instance, and the dwadasamsa (D-12) of the father. The book also reads transits in the rasi chart against a varga's ashtakavarga, and the other way round.

## Parasara, not Varahamihira {#parasara}

Varahamihira, a later astrologer (around 600 AD), gives the same tables but for three disagreements, in each of which a benefic and a malefic cell trade places (§12.8):

1. **Moon's table, from the Moon and from Mars.** Parasara: the Moon is benefic in the 9th from the Moon and malefic in the 9th from Mars. Varahamihira: the reverse.
2. **Moon's table, from Jupiter.** Parasara: the Moon is benefic in the 2nd from Jupiter and malefic in the 12th. Varahamihira: the reverse.
3. **Venus's table, from Mars.** Parasara: Venus is benefic in the 4th from Mars and malefic in the 5th. Varahamihira: the reverse.

The book follows Parasara, because his way of stating the tables resists corruption. For each house he gives the number of references from which a planet is malefic, then names them, then names the benefic ones as well: three statements that must agree, like a checksum. A plain list can be changed by swapping one word for another of the same metre, without anything showing.

hora follows Parasara too, with no option for Varahamihira: the book follows Parasara, and chart 6's printed answers come out right only with his tables: every combination of Varahamihira's cells breaks some of them (ADR 0015). The book itself leaves the question open until the use of the pindas in timing has been researched.

## Whole rasis, not bhava chalit {#whole-rasis}

Some astrologers cast ashtakavarga from a bhaava chakra or chalit chakra, with houses divided by degrees (Sripati's or Porphyry's method), so that a planet a few degrees past the lagna's rasi may count as in the 1st house. They do so only from the lagna, though, and not from the seven other references, which the book calls neither one thing nor the other (§12.8). Here a house counted from any reference is one whole rasi, the 1st being the reference's own, as everywhere in hora ([bhava](/en/kb/bhava), ADR 0010). With the lagna at 28° Cancer and Mars at 2° Leo, Mars is in the 2nd from the lagna though only 4° away.

## What hora does not compute {#not-computed}

hora shows the natal tables. The uses the book builds on them are left to you:

- **Transits.** Ashtakavarga's main use (§12.1, chapter 25): judging a planet moving through a rasi by the rekhas that rasi has in the planet's natal BAV and in the natal SAV.
- **Muhurta.** Choosing a time for a venture: the rasis holding the lagna, the Moon and the Sun at that time should have 30 rekhas or more in the native's SAV (§12.4).
- **Vaastu.** In the Vedic science of architecture, the rasis with the most rekhas in a planet's SoAV point to the direction for the rooms of that planet's activities: Venus for the bedroom, say, or Mercury for the study (§12.7 footnote 45).
- **Timing with the pindas.** See [sodhya pinda](/en/kb/sodhya-pinda).

One printed answer carries a mark the book never explains: the Moon's 5 in Pisces in Exercise 19, printed "5\*". The 5 fits both the row's total and the SAV in Pisces, so hora takes it as 5.

## In the chart

The chart page's *Ashtakavarga* section has the tables for the rasi chart and, folded under its name, for the varga chakra shown. Each is counted from that chart's own lagna, whatever the chart page counts bhavas from. The first table gives each rasi with its bhava, then a row for each planet's BAV, the SAV, and last the lagna's BAV. Counts in the favourable or strong band are marked +, those in the unfavourable or weak band −, and a dot marks the rasi a planet is in. Below it come the SoAV and the pindas, explained on the [sodhya pinda](/en/kb/sodhya-pinda) page, and a folded PAV for each planet and the lagna.
