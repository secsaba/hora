# Yogas

A yoga (combination) is a particular arrangement of [grahas](/en/kb/graha) (planets), [bhavas](/en/kb/bhava) (houses) or their lords in a [chart](/en/kb/chart), to which the classics tie a particular result (§11.1). The general tools of the earlier chapters, the [vargas](/en/kb/vargas), bhavas, [karakas](/en/kb/karakas), [arudha padas](/en/kb/arudha-padas), [aspects and argalas](/en/kb/aspects-and-argalas), describe a chart in broad strokes; a yoga picks out one pattern and says what it gives. It is not the [Sun-Moon yoga](/en/kb/sun-moon-yoga), a limb of the panchaanga that shares the word.

The book lists only a selection and urges reading further in the classics. It also warns that programs which list yogas sometimes get them wrong, and that an astrologer should know the important ones and be able to judge them without software. hora lists them for you to check, not to accept.

## The classes

The book sorts its yogas into nine classes, for convenience. They are on six pages:

- [Ravi and Chandra yogas](/en/kb/ravi-and-chandra-yogas): combinations around the Sun and around the Moon (§11.2–11.3).
- [Mahaapurusha yogas](/en/kb/mahaapurusha-yogas): five kinds of great person, one for each element (§11.4).
- [Naabhasa yogas](/en/kb/naabhasa-yogas): patterns of the whole sky, by kind of rasi, by kendras, by shape and by count (§11.5).
- [Other popular yogas](/en/kb/other-popular-yogas): Subha to Vimala, each with its own rule (§11.6).
- [Raaja and Raaja Sambandha yogas](/en/kb/raaja-yogas): power, and nearness to those in power (§11.7–11.8).
- [Dhana and Daridra yogas](/en/kb/dhana-and-daridra-yogas): wealth and poverty (§11.9–11.10).

Each page gives every yoga's rule in its own words and the results the classics ascribe to it, as they ascribe them, harsh ones included. The book's caveats below apply to all of them, so the pages do not repeat them yoga by yoga.

## Reading a yoga in a varga {#in-a-varga}

Many yogas apply in a [varga chakra](/en/kb/varga-chakra) (divisional chart) as well as in the rasi chart. There the results are read by what that chart signifies (§11.1, §11.11). The book's own example: the Sun and Mercury together in Leo in the dasamsa (D-10) give a strong Budha-Aaditya yoga for one's career, felt all through life and above all in the dasas of the Sun, Mercury and Leo. The book points some classes at some charts, such as the Ravi yogas at the D-9 and D-10, and keeps others mainly to the rasi chart.

The results of most yogas come chiefly in the dasas (periods) of the grahas and rasis taking part. Naabhasa yogas are the exception: they act in every dasa (§11.5).

## What the book asks of a yoga {#strong}

For a yoga to be fully present, every condition must hold and the grahas taking part must be strong (§11.6). The book gives no measure of that strength here. It also allows that a dasa can bring some of a yoga's results when not every condition is met.

## The book's caveats {#caveats}

The chapter closes with four cautions (§11.11):

- **Read results in today's terms.** The classics speak of kings and armies. One with Kamala yoga, the book's own example, may not become a king but may lead a government or a state. In a varga chakra, adapt the results to that chart's matters.
- **A weak yoga gives less.** The great results ascribed to a yoga may not come about if it is weak. Only a very strong yoga can be expected to give all of them.
- **One yoga rarely decides a life.** A single yoga does not make or break a person unless it is very powerful.
- **Partial conditions, partial results.** Where a yoga has many conditions, some of its results may come even if not all of them hold.

## What "to judge" means {#to-judge}

hora checks each rule mechanically and shows a yoga in one of three states:

- **Present:** every condition hora can measure holds.
- **To judge:** every condition it can measure holds, and the rest are ones the book states but gives no measure for, such as a graha being strong. Each such condition is named in words on the row, for you to weigh.
- **Absent:** a measurable condition fails. Absent yogas are not listed.

A partial yoga is not listed, despite the last caveat: nearly every chart would show dozens. The one exception is Raaja yoga §11.7.3 (1), whose two halves are each a raja yoga by themselves, so its row says which held. hora never rates a yoga or weighs strength, and it does not list results on the chart page, only each yoga and how it forms.

## How hora reads the book's gaps {#readings}

Where the book is explicit, hora follows it. Where it is silent or contradicts itself, these readings apply (ADR 0014):

- **Which charts.** Each yoga is checked in the rasi chart and in the varga chakra shown, counted from that chart's lagna, whatever the chart page counts bhavas from. Yogas that mix charts or need the rasi chart's degrees are checked only there: Kalpadruma, Vishnu, Gouri, Chandikaa, Bhaarathi, Vidyut, Jaya, Raaja yoga §11.7.3 (9) and Daridra yogas §11.10 (8) and (9).
- **Rahu and Ketu.** "A planet" or "all the planets" means the seven planets, so the nodes give no yoga from the 2nd or 12th of the Sun or Moon and do not count in Kemadruma. A "malefic" includes them, as in §3.2.2, and they count wherever a rule names them or as the lord of a rasi.
- **Mercury's nature.** Mercury is a [saumya graha](/en/kb/graha-characteristics#saumya-graha) (natural benefic) or a kroora graha (natural malefic) by his company in the chart being read. When his company is evenly balanced the book gives no answer, so hora tries both: if they agree that is the result, and if they differ the yoga is to judge.
- **A rasi's lord.** Scorpio and Aquarius each have two lords, and either can form a yoga. If one of them is in the rasi, the other alone counts as its lord. The row names the graha.
- **"Occupied by".** A place occupied by benefics may hold others too, unless the rule says "only". A set of places that benefics occupy must between them hold every natural benefic.
- **Deep exaltation and combustion.** These exist only in the rasi chart. Where a rule asks for a graha in deep exaltation, hora checks the uchcha rasi and leaves the degree to judge. Where it asks for a graha not combust, that is to judge when the graha shares the Sun's rasi, and does not apply in a varga chakra.
- **Not computed.** Raaja yoga §11.7.3 (18) says only when other raja yogas work better, and Daridra yoga §11.10 (10) depends on "benefic houses" the chapter does not define. Both are on their pages, but never on a chart.

## What was checked against the book's charts {#checked}

Later chapters state about a dozen of these yogas on the book's own example charts, and those statements are pinned in hora's tests, counted from the lagna and in the rasi chart unless said otherwise. A row hora lists beyond what the book states is expected: the book does not say those yogas are absent. All the charts open from the [book's charts](/en/kb/book-charts) appendix.

**Reproduced:**

- Chart 9: Kalpadruma, by the Sun, Saturn, Venus and Saturn again (pp. 128–129).
- Chart 10: Maalavya, Sasa and Ruchaka, and the raja yogas of Venus with Saturn and of the Moon with Mercury (p. 137).
- Chart 14: the raja yoga of the Sun and Jupiter, with Venus in a pair with each of them, since the book notes only that functional malefics are with them (pp. 136–137).
- Charts 6 and 11: Bhadra (p. 152).
- Charts 17, 19 and 20: Vesi, by Mercury, Mercury and Venus (pp. 217, 218, 221). The book's remark on chart 20 that Venus is the lagna lord is a slip, and the Vesi stands.
- Chart 61: Budha-Aaditya in Scorpio (p. 412).
- Chart 13: a raja yoga of the Sun and Jupiter in the 10th (p. 172), and in its vimsamsa (D-20) Raaja yoga §11.7.3 (12) of the Moon and Mars in Virgo, and Budha-Aaditya in the 2nd (p. 174).
- Chart 18: in its saptamsa (D-7), the raja yoga of Rahu as 5th lord with Venus (p. 218).
- Chart 3: Raaja yoga §11.7.3 (1) by its atma karaka Mercury and putra karaka Saturn alone, its lagna and 5th lords not being conjoined (p. 265).
- Chart 6: the Vipareeta Raaja yoga of Mars, lord of the 3rd and 8th, with the Sun, lord of the 12th, to judge (p. 230).
- Chart 90: in its dasamsa (D-10), the 8th and 12th lords Jupiter and the Moon together in Scorpio, to judge; the book calls that yoga ineffective, the Moon being debilitated in a kendra (p. 494).
- Closeness: chart 10's Venus and Saturn within 1° and its Moon and Mercury within 6°, chart 14's Sun and Jupiter more than 8° apart, chart 6's Mars and Sun 17′ apart.
- Dasa varga amsas: chart 14's Sun and Jupiter in simhaasanaamsa, chart 13's Jupiter in uttamaamsa, chart 10's Mars in simhaasanaamsa and its Moon and Mercury below paarijaataamsa.

**Where hora differs from the book:**

- Chart 10's Venus and chart 6's Mercury are printed as uttamaamsa (pp. 137, 230). hora counts two good vargas for each, paarijaataamsa, and no reading of the vargas found gives three. As with the hora and ghati lagnas, the cause stays open.
- Rama's chart (p. 123): the book counts his seven planets in six rasis and names Daama yoga. hora lists Sarpa for that chart, to judge, with Saturn, Mars and the Sun in the 4th, 7th and 10th and Jupiter and the Moon in the 1st. A Sankhya yoga applies only when no other Naabhasa yoga does (§11.5.4), so the book's own proviso stops the Daama. The test pins the count of six, not a Daama.
- Gola yoga is never listed. All seven planets in one rasi always give an Aasraya yoga too, which takes precedence over the Sankhya yogas.

**Not checked:** chart 14's Sasa in its drekkana (D-3), where Libra is adopted as the lagna for a brother (p. 176); the raja yogas of charts 24 and 69 counted from a dasa lagna or dasa rasi (pp. 241, 412); chart 13's raja yoga with the ghati lagna's lord and chart 90's yogada on the ghati and hora lagnas, which hora cannot reproduce from the book's printed lagnas (pp. 174, 496); and chart 16's "Bhadra yoga like results" (p. 179).

## In the chart

The chart page's *Yogas* section has a table for the rasi chart and, folded under its name, one for the varga chakra shown. Rows are grouped by class in the book's order. Each names the yoga, its grahas with their roles and bhavas, and anything to judge. Raaja yoga rows in the rasi chart also show how close the two grahas are and each one's dasa varga [amsa](/en/kb/amsabala#dasa-varga).
