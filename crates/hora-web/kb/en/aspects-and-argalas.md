# Aspects and argalas

A [graha](/en/kb/graha) (planet) acts on more than the [rasi](/en/kb/rasi) (sign) it stands in. The book describes three ways it does so: two kinds of drishti (aspect) and argala (intervention). This page gives the rules for each, how they differ in weight, how to read them together, and where the chart page shows them.

## Graha drishti {#graha-drishti}

Graha drishti (planetary aspect) belongs to the graha itself (§10.2). Each of the seven planets aspects the 7th rasi from where it stands, counting its own rasi as 1. Three of them reach further: Saturn also aspects the 3rd and 10th, Mars the 4th and 8th, and Jupiter the 5th and 9th.

The aspect falls on the whole rasi, and so on the [bhava](/en/kb/bhava) (house) it makes and on every graha in it, whatever their degrees. Mars in Pisces, for instance, aspects Gemini, Virgo and Libra, and any graha in them. It need not be returned: Venus in Libra is aspected by that Mars, but her own graha drishti falls on Aries, not on Pisces.

The section opens by saying all planets aspect the 7th, yet the book's two exercises treat the nodes differently: the answer on graha drishti covers the seven planets, and the one on rasi drishti adds Rahu and Ketu. The aspects it later gives Rahu are all ones rasi drishti explains, and at least one does not fall on the 7th. hora follows that reading: Rahu and Ketu have no graha drishti.

## Rasi drishti {#rasi-drishti}

Rasi drishti (sign aspect) is an aspect between the rasis themselves (§10.3). The rule goes by a rasi's [kind](/en/kb/rasi-characteristics#chara-sthira-dwiswabhava). Chara (movable) and sthira (fixed) rasis aspect each other, each leaving out the one next to it. Dwiswabhava (dual) rasis aspect one another.

So Cancer aspects Taurus, Scorpio and Aquarius, but not its neighbour Leo. Scorpio aspects Aries, Cancer and Capricorn, but not its neighbour Libra. Sagittarius aspects Gemini, Virgo and Pisces. The aspect always works both ways. All nine grahas, Rahu and Ketu included, aspect whatever their rasi aspects: those rasis, their bhavas and the grahas in them.

### How the two differ

Rasi drishti comes from where a graha is, graha drishti from what it is (§10.4). Grahas sharing a rasi therefore all reach the same rasis by rasi drishti, though each brings its own nature to them. By graha drishti they may reach quite different places, with the 7th the only one the seven planets all have in common. The two rules are also separate: only a dwiswabhava rasi aspects its own 7th by rasi drishti, since the 7th from a chara or sthira rasi is of its own kind.

The book also ranks these influences by weight (§10.5). Rasi drishti is the slightest, graha drishti firmer, and argala the one that settles things.

## Argala {#argala}

Argala (intervention) is the word for a bolt, and a graha with argala on a rasi has a say in how that rasi's matters are settled (§10.5). Counted from the rasi receiving it, primary argala comes from the 2nd, 4th and 11th. A graha in the 4th from Taurus, which is Leo, causes argala on Taurus. It also falls on the bhava there and on the grahas in it.

An argala by a [saumya graha](/en/kb/graha-characteristics#saumya-graha) (natural benefic) is a subhaargala (benefic intervention), and one by a kroora graha (natural malefic) a paapaargala (malefic intervention). hora does not print these two words. Mercury's nature depends on his company, the book does not say in which chart to judge that or how a tie goes, and no value here depends on it.

A bhava can cause argala too, with or without a graha in it: the matters of the 2nd, 4th and 11th from a bhava take part in that bhava's matters. hora lists only grahas, so this stays a way of reasoning. Equally, argala on a graha is argala on its rasi: count from the rasi it stands in, and every graha there receives the same. This lets you ask about a matter through its [karaka](/en/kb/karakas) (significator), and the same goes for a lagna (ascendant) or an [arudha pada](/en/kb/arudha-padas) (the rasi that shows how a matter appears).

### Secondary argala {#secondary-argala}

The 5th from a rasi adds a secondary argala (§10.5).

### What each one shows

Each source plays its own part in the matter (§10.7). Here is the 10th bhava, the work one is known for, as an example:

| Argala from | What it shows | From the 10th bhava |
|---|---|---|
| 2nd | What keeps the matter alive | The 11th: earnings keep a career going |
| 4th | What sets its condition and direction | The 1st: one's own vigour and character steer it |
| 11th | What sets gains from it in motion | The 8th: a windfall may be what lets a venture take off |
| 5th (secondary) | What adds to it from the side | The 2nd: family and savings help it along |

## Virodhargala {#virodhargala}

Virodhargala (obstruction) is what stands against an argala (§10.6). Each source of argala is paired with one rasi, its mirror image across the rasi receiving it: the 3rd obstructs the 11th, the 9th the 5th, the 10th the 4th and the 12th the 2nd.

An argala with nothing in its paired rasi is unobstructed. A graha in an obstructing rasi whose paired rasi is empty has nothing there to obstruct.

**Ketu's reversal.** If the rasi receiving the argala holds Ketu, both argala and virodhargala on it are counted anti-zodiacally. Only the rasi holding Ketu is counted this way: Ketu causing argala on another rasi changes nothing about that rasi.

**Several malefics in the 3rd.** The book adds a principle: when several kroora grahas gather in the 3rd, they change sides and count as argala. It gives no number, and its own answer to Exercise 16 still lists two of them in a 3rd as virodhargala. A threshold of our own would silently move grahas from one side to the other, so hora does not apply the principle. It lists whoever is in the 3rd as virodhargala, and leaves it to you to bear the principle in mind when several kroora grahas stand there.

## Reading them

The book's method starts from a question, not from the table (§10.7): the matter asked about fixes a bhava or a karaka, and only what falls on that point is weighed. Its test is a count of the grahas on each side, with strength as the tie-break. The count does not finish the reading, since what the influence means comes from the rasis, bhavas and grahas themselves.

hora shows both sides and gives no verdict. The book does not say whether to count pair by pair or over all, has no rule for the strengths in a tie, and in its own example of Saturn's argalas does not stop to apply the count. A computed verdict would look more certain than the book is, so each argala stands beside its obstruction for you to weigh.

## A worked example

Take hora's chart for 1 January 2000 at 06:00 UT, at Greenwich, with bhavas counted from Saturn ([see the chart](/en/chart?date=2000-01-01&time=06%3A00&calendar=gregorian&utc=stated&offset=%2B00%3A00&latitude=51.48&longitude=0&altitude=&ayanamsa=lahiri&node=mean&sunrise=upper-limb-apparent&reference=saturn&style=south)). The book reads work and livelihood through Saturn, so argala on him shows what decides them. The lagna is in Scorpio, with Venus. Saturn and Jupiter are in Aries, Rahu in Cancer, the Moon in Libra, the Sun and Mercury in Sagittarius, Ketu in Capricorn and Mars in Aquarius.

1. **Graha drishti.** Saturn aspects the 3rd, 7th and 10th from Aries: Gemini, Libra and Capricorn, reaching the Moon and Ketu. Jupiter, in the same rasi, aspects the 5th, 7th and 9th: Leo, Libra and Sagittarius, reaching the Moon, the Sun and Mercury. Only Libra is common to both. The Moon is the one graha whose graha drishti falls on Saturn.
2. **Rasi drishti.** Aries is chara, so it aspects Leo, Scorpio and Aquarius. Saturn and Jupiter both reach Venus and Mars this way, and those two aspect them in return.
3. **Argala on Saturn.** Aries is the first row of the table. Taurus and Pisces, the 2nd and 12th, are empty. Rahu in Cancer causes argala from the 4th, and Ketu in Capricorn, the 10th, obstructs it. Leo, the 5th, is empty, so the Sun and Mercury in Sagittarius, the 9th, have nothing to obstruct. Mars in Aquarius causes argala from the 11th, and Gemini, the 3rd, is empty, so it is unobstructed.
4. **A reading.** Mars and Rahu are both kroora grahas, so these are paapaargalas. Mars, free of any obstruction, suggests that energy and competition are what bring gains from this person's work. Rahu's restlessness drives the work's condition, with Ketu standing against it.
5. **Why there is no verdict.** Counted over all, two grahas cause argala and three stand in obstructing rasis, so obstruction is ahead. Counted pair by pair, Rahu and Ketu balance, Mars is free and the Sun and Mercury block nothing, so argala is ahead. The book does not choose between these counts, and neither does hora.
6. **Ketu's reversal.** Capricorn holds Ketu, so its row is marked with a star and counted backwards. Its 2nd is Sagittarius, its 4th Libra, its 5th Virgo and its 11th Pisces, so the Sun and Mercury and the Moon cause argala on it. Its 12th is Aquarius and its 10th Aries, so Mars obstructs the argala of the Sun and Mercury, and Jupiter and Saturn that of the Moon. Venus, in its 3rd, Scorpio, has nothing to obstruct, since Pisces is empty.
7. **The reference point.** Choosing the lagna instead moves Aries to the 6th row, since it is the 6th from Scorpio. The grahas in the row stay the same: the reference point changes only the bhava numbers and the order of the rows.

## In the chart

The chart page has two sections, *Aspects* and *Argalas*. Each gives a table for the rasi chart and, folded under its name, one for the varga chakra (divisional chart) shown.

*Aspects* lists, for each graha, the rasis it aspects by each kind of drishti, with the bhava of each in brackets and the grahas in them on the line below. *Argalas* has a row for each rasi, with each source and its paired rasi side by side. A star marks a rasi counted in reverse. Only the nine grahas count here, as causes and as those aspected, never a lagna, a special lagna or an arudha pada. Both drishtis fall on whole rasis, with no orb and no measure of strength.

To see the argala on a graha, the arudha lagna (AL) or the ghati lagna (GL), choose it in the *Count bhavas from* field on the home page: its rasi becomes the first row, in each chart its own.
