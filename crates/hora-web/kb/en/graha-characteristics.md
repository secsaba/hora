# Graha characteristics

If a chart is a play, the [rasis](/en/kb/rasi) (signs) are its stage and the [grahas](/en/kb/graha) (planets) its cast: each graha stands for people in the native's life. Like the [rasis](/en/kb/rasi-characteristics), each graha carries a set of qualities. This page goes through them one list at a time, in the book's order, then gathers them graha by graha. Each table lists every value with the grahas that have it; a graha's name takes you to its own section further down.

Seven of the nine are real planets. Rahu and Ketu are chaayaa grahas (shadow planets), points found by calculation, and many lists leave them out. Where the book gives a graha no value, the table shows "—".

## Avatara {#avatara}

Vishnu came to Earth many times, each time in a new form, an avatara (incarnation). Nine of these forms drew their divine essence from one graha each, which links the grahas to Vishnu as the [limbs](/en/kb/rasi-characteristics#limb) link the rasis to him. The story of an avatara is a way into the nature of its graha.

{{table:graha-avatara}}

## Saumya and kroora {#saumya-graha}

A saumya graha (natural benefic) inclines by its own nature to do good, a kroora graha (natural malefic) to do harm, before anything particular to a chart is weighed. What a graha gives in a chart starts from this inborn nature.

Jupiter and Venus are saumya. The Sun, Mars, Saturn, Rahu and Ketu are kroora. Two grahas change sides. Mercury takes on the nature of his company: on his own, or joined by more saumya than kroora grahas, he is saumya; joined by more kroora ones, he is kroora. The Moon follows her phase: saumya while she grows in Sukla paksha (the bright half of the lunar month), kroora while she shrinks in Krishna paksha (the dark half). The book's list in this chapter leaves Saturn out; it counts him among the natural malefics in chapter 13, and hora follows that.

{{table:graha-saumya-graha}}

## Main governance {#governance}

Each of the seven planets chiefly looks after one part of a person, from the soul to grief. The book names none for Rahu and Ketu.

{{table:graha-governance}}

## Colour {#colour}

Each of the seven planets shows a colour, which a reading can draw on whenever the colour of something has to be judged. The Sun and Mars share one, and so do the Moon and Jupiter. The book gives Rahu and Ketu none.

{{table:graha-colour}}

## Cabinet {#cabinet}

The grahas form a royal court. The Sun and the Moon reign side by side, Mars commands the army, Mercury is the heir, Jupiter and Venus advise, Saturn serves, and Rahu and Ketu are the troops. A graha's rank hints at the part its person plays among others.

{{table:graha-cabinet}}

## Deity {#deity}

Each of the seven planets is placed under a deity of the Hindu pantheon, whose character helps explain the graha's. The book gives none for Rahu and Ketu.

{{table:graha-deity}}

## Sex {#sex}

Each of the seven planets is male or female, which matters, for one, when a chart is asked about children. The book counts Mercury and Saturn as female. Other texts call these two neuter; hora follows the book. Rahu and Ketu are given no sex.

{{table:graha-sex}}

## Element {#element}

The five [elements](/en/kb/rasi-characteristics#element) met among the rasis each have a graha as ruler: Mars rules agni (fire), Mercury bhoo (earth), Saturn vaayu (air), Jupiter aakaasa (ether) and Venus jala (water). The Sun shares the fiery nature of Mars, the Moon the watery nature of Venus. Ether, which no rasi has alone, belongs to Jupiter alone. A graha's element shows the grain of its nature, much as it does for a rasi. Rahu and Ketu have none.

{{table:graha-element}}

## Varna {#varna}

The four [varnas](/en/kb/rasi-characteristics#varna) (classes) sort the grahas too. Read a graha's varna as a temperament: what its person is best at and reaches for first. Rahu and Ketu are given none.

{{table:graha-varna}}

## Guna {#guna}

Each of the seven planets has one of the three [gunas](/en/kb/rasi-characteristics#guna) (qualities). Sattwa is easily mistaken for mildness. What it asks for is a pure motive, not a soft manner, so even a fierce graha can be saattwik. Rahu and Ketu are given no guna.

{{table:graha-guna}}

## Abode {#abode}

Each graha is pictured at home in one kind of place, an image that sums up its character. The book gives no abode for Mars, nor for Rahu and Ketu.

{{table:graha-abode}}

## Sapta dhaatu {#sapta-dhaatu}

The body is built of the sapta dhaatu (seven matters), and each of the seven planets rules one of them. A graha in trouble in a chart can show trouble with its matter.

{{table:graha-sapta-dhaatu}}

## Time period {#time-period}

Each of the seven planets rules a span of time, from a minute to a year. The spans serve mainly in prasna, the branch of astrology that answers a question from a chart of the moment it is asked. An ayana is half a year and a ritu a season of two months; both have their own sections below. Rahu and Ketu rule no span.

{{table:graha-time-period}}

## Taste {#taste}

Each of the seven planets governs a taste. The grahas that act on the [bhava](/en/kb/bhava) (house) of food can show which taste someone prefers, and in a stretch of life when a graha threatens health, its taste is one to eat sparingly. Rahu and Ketu govern none.

{{table:graha-taste}}

## Digbala {#digbala}

Each of the seven planets is strongest in one direction: its digbala (directional strength). In a chart the four directions are four bhavas: east is the lagna (ascendant), south the 10th bhava at the top of the sky, west the 7th and north the 4th, beneath the Earth. The book comes back to digbala in later chapters. Rahu and Ketu have none.

{{table:graha-digbala}}

## Day and night {#day-and-night}

Like the [divaa and nishaa rasis](/en/kb/rasi-characteristics#divaa-rasi), the seven planets are strong either by day or by night, except Mercury, who is strong at all times. Rahu and Ketu are given neither.

{{table:graha-day-and-night}}

## Paksha {#paksha}

A [paksha](/en/kb/tithi) is half a lunar month: Sukla paksha while the Moon waxes, Krishna paksha while she wanes. Saumya grahas are strong in Sukla paksha, kroora grahas in Krishna paksha. The book's rule reaches Mercury and the Moon only through their changing nature, so hora shows no paksha for them. The book counts Rahu and Ketu among the kroora grahas, but hora gives this strength, like the next, to the seven planets only, so the nodes show none.

{{table:graha-paksha}}

## Ayana {#ayana}

By the Sun's course the year has two ayanas (halves): Uttara ayana (north course) while the Sun moves from Capricorn to Gemini, and Dakshina ayana (south course) while it moves from Cancer to Sagittarius. Saumya grahas are strong in Uttara ayana, kroora grahas in Dakshina ayana. The same grahas go without as in the paksha table.

{{table:graha-ayana}}

## Ritu {#ritu}

The year has six ritus (seasons) of two months each, from vasanta (spring) to sisira, and six of the seven planets rule one each. The book gives none to the Sun, nor to Rahu and Ketu.

{{table:graha-ritu}}

## Dhatu, moola, jeeva {#dhatu-moola-jeeva}

All nine grahas, Rahu and Ketu included, rule one of three kinds of things: dhatu (metals and materials), moola (roots and vegetables) or jeeva (living beings). This dhatu is a different word from the sapta dhaatu above.

{{table:graha-dhatu-moola-jeeva}}
