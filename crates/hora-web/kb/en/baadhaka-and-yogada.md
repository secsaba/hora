# Baadhakas and yogadas

Two kinds of graha stand out in a chart whatever their [functional nature](/en/kb/functional-nature): a **yogada**, which brings good luck in money or in power, and a **baadhaka**, which brings trouble to a bhava (§13.2–13.3).

## Yogada {#yogada}

A yogada (giver of yoga) is a graha linked to the lagna and to one of two [special lagnas](/en/kb/special-lagna) at once. The [hora lagna](/en/kb/special-lagna#hora-lagna) (HL) shows wealth and the [ghati lagna](/en/kb/special-lagna#ghati-lagna) (GL) power, so:

- a graha linked to both HL and the lagna is a yogada for money;
- a graha linked to both GL and the lagna is a yogada for power and authority.

A graha is linked to a rasi when it owns it, is in it, or aspects it. A yogada brings good fortune in its matters whatever its functional nature: even a functional malefic can be one. The book adds that grahas taking part in important [yogas](/en/kb/yogas) bring good luck in the same way.

### Rasi drishti only {#rasi-drishti}

The book's rule says only "aspects", which could mean either kind of aspect. Every time it works a yogada out in a chart, though, the aspect is [rasi drishti](/en/kb/aspects-and-argalas#rasi-drishti) (pp. 230, 495, 496), never [graha drishti](/en/kb/aspects-and-argalas#graha-drishti), and reading graha drishti as well would make yogadas of grahas the book passes over (chart 90's Saturn, for money). So hora counts rasi drishti only (ADR 0016).

### How hora reads it {#reading}

- Only the seven planets can be yogadas, and a rasi's owner is its seven-planet lord, so Saturn owns Aquarius and Mars owns Scorpio.
- Every graha the rule gives is listed, with each of its links named, even where the book names only one of them.
- In a varga chakra, the varga's own lagna, HL and GL are used. The page's reference point plays no part: a yogada is always about the lagna.
- HL and GL are counted from sunrise. Where the chart has no Vedic day, it has no HL or GL, and so no yogada.

A made-up case: the lagna is in Aries and HL in Leo. The Sun owns Leo, so the Sun in Aries, owning one and in the other, is a yogada for money. So is Mars, the lord of Aries, if he is in Leo. And so is any of the seven planets in Leo, since Leo, a fixed rasi, has rasi drishti on Aries.

## Baadhaka sthaana {#baadhaka-sthaana}

Every bhava has a **baadhaka sthaana** (troubling spot): a rasi counted from the bhava's own rasi, by whether that rasi is [movable, fixed or dual](/en/kb/rasi-characteristics#chara-sthira-dwiswabhava):

- from a chara (movable) rasi, Aries, Cancer, Libra or Capricorn, the 11th;
- from a sthira (fixed) rasi, Taurus, Leo, Scorpio or Aquarius, the 9th;
- from a dwiswabhava (dual) rasi, Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius or Pisces, the 7th.

The lord of the baadhaka sthaana is the **baadhaka** (troublemaker) of that bhava. Aquarius and Scorpio have two lords, and both are baadhakas there, as the book's Table 31 prints them: the baadhakas of Aries, whose sthaana is Aquarius, are Saturn and Rahu, and those of Capricorn, whose sthaana is Scorpio, Mars and Ketu.

The dasas of a bhava's baadhaka, and of the grahas in its baadhaka sthaana, can bring obstacles and trouble to that bhava's matters. Say the 4th bhava, home and comforts, is in Leo. Leo is fixed, so its baadhaka sthaana is the 9th from it, Aries, and Mars is the baadhaka of the 4th: his periods, and those of whatever stands in Aries, can disturb one's peace at home. With the lagna in Sagittarius, a dual rasi, the baadhaka sthaana of the lagna is the 7th, Gemini, and its baadhaka Mercury.

This works from any bhava and from any [arudha pada](/en/kb/arudha-padas#bhava-arudha), in any [varga chakra](/en/kb/vargas): the bhava of a matter in the varga that shows it, or the arudha pada that shows how the matter appears in the world. An arudha pada in a rasi has that rasi's baadhaka sthaana.

## On the chart page {#chart-page}

The *Interpreting charts* topic shows both. *Baadhakas* is a table of the twelve bhavas, counted from the page's reference point, for the rasi chart and for the varga chakra shown: each bhava's rasi, the bhava arudhas that fall in it, its baadhaka sthaana and the bhava that rasi is, its baadhakas, and the grahas in the sthaana. *Yogadas* lists each yogada of the two charts, what it gives, and how it is linked to the lagna and to HL or GL.
