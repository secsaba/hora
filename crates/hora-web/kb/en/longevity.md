# Longevity

How long will I live? The question is old and common, and chapter 14 answers it with two methods that place a life in one of three longevity categories: short life, up to 36 years; middle life, 36 to 72; and long life, 72 to 108. They rest on the tools of the [marakas and Rudra](/en/kb/marakas-and-rudra) page, and the book's later chapters build on both to time death with dasas.

## A caution {#caution}

The book is plain about how this knowledge may be used (§14.1, §14.6). An ethical astrologer does not frighten anyone with a predicted death. At most, before a difficult period, they warn gently and suggest remedies that may ward the danger off. The book also sees a fair use in matchmaking, where two partners should have comparable spans of life.

Neither method is infallible. There are exceptions the book does not cover, and it says outright that one should not be swayed by these calculations: in its own exercise, one of the two methods gets the answer wrong. Nor should the knowledge be turned to fame or to scaring people; the sages warned that whoever misuses astrology lives in misery.

hora shows the categories in the book's own terms because the book teaches them as calculations and builds on them. A category on the chart page is the output of a rule, not a prediction.

## The method of three pairs {#three-pairs}

This method gives the rough range of a life (§14.4). It looks at three pairs of grahas or points:

1. the lagna's lord and the 8th lord, the 8th found by [Table 32](/en/kb/marakas-and-rudra#table-32) as for Rudra, or counted normally with [Saturn in the lagna or the 7th](/en/kb/marakas-and-rudra#saturn);
2. the Moon and Saturn;
3. the lagna and the [hora lagna](/en/kb/special-lagna#hora-lagna) (HL).

Each pair is judged by whether its two rasis are [movable, fixed or dual](/en/kb/rasi-characteristics#chara-sthira-dwiswabhava), by the book's Table 33:

- **long life:** fixed with dual, or both movable;
- **middle life:** movable with fixed, or both dual;
- **short life:** movable with dual, or both fixed.

For a Scorpio or Aquarius lagna or 8th, the lord is the [stronger lord](/en/kb/arudha-padas#stronger-lord).

### Combining the pairs {#combining}

- If two pairs agree, or all three, their category is the result.
- If all three differ, the third pair, the lagna and HL, decides.
- But if all three differ and the Moon is in the lagna or the 7th, the second pair, the Moon and Saturn, decides.

HL is counted from sunrise. Where a chart has no Vedic day, as near the poles, it has no HL, so no third pair and no combined result; the first two pairs are still shown.

A made-up case: the lagna is in Gemini, a dual rasi, and its lord Mercury in Virgo, also dual. By Table 32 the 8th from Gemini is Capricorn, and its lord Saturn is in Taurus, fixed: dual with fixed, long life. The Moon in Aries, movable, with Saturn in fixed Taurus gives middle life. HL in Leo, fixed, with the dual lagna gives long life again. Two pairs say long, so the life is long.

## Paramaayush {#paramaayush}

When two pairs agree, Parasara's further hint gives the longest life the chart allows, the paramaayush (§14.4, Table 34). The category of the two agreeing pairs sets a figure at the top of its range: 36 years for short life, 72 for middle, 108 for long. That figure holds when the third pair shows middle life. If the third shows short life, a ninth comes off it; if long life, a ninth is added, whatever the other two show. So two pairs of middle life with a third of short life give 64 years, all three pairs of long life give 120, and in the case above, two of long life with a third of middle life give 108. When all three pairs differ there is no paramaayush.

## The eighth lord method {#eighth-lord}

Parasara and Jaimini give a second method (§14.5). Take the [stronger](/en/kb/arudha-padas#stronger-rasi) of the lagna and the 7th as the reference, and find the lord of the 8th from it, counted in the normal way, not by Table 32. Where he stands from the reference gives the category:

- in a [kendra](/en/kb/bhava-categories#kendra) (1st, 4th, 7th or 10th): long life;
- in a [panaphara](/en/kb/bhava-categories#panaphara) (2nd, 5th, 8th or 11th): middle life;
- in an [apoklima](/en/kb/bhava-categories#apoklima) (3rd, 6th, 9th or 12th): short life.

A made-up case: Leo is the reference, so the 8th is Pisces and its lord Jupiter. Jupiter in Scorpio, the 4th from Leo, shows long life; in Virgo, the 2nd, middle life; in Libra, the 3rd, short life.

## Chart 8 {#chart-8}

The book's Exercise 23 applies both methods to [chart 8](/en/chart?calendar=gregorian&date=1946-12-02&time=06:45:00&utc=stated&offset=%2B01:00:00&latitude=38.1&longitude=15.65&altitude=0&ayanamsa=lahiri&node=mean&sunrise=upper-limb-apparent&topic=longevity) (p. 186). The lagna is Scorpio and its lord in Scorpio, fixed; the 8th lord by Table 32 is Jupiter in Libra, movable: middle life. The Moon in Aquarius, fixed, with Saturn in Cancer, movable: middle life. The lagna and HL both in Scorpio, fixed: short life. Two pairs agree, so the result is middle life, and by the rule above the paramaayush is 64 years. The native died at 50.

By the eighth lord method, Scorpio is stronger than Taurus, and its 8th lord counted normally is Mercury, in Libra, the 12th from Scorpio, an apoklima: short life. The book says plainly that this method did not work here, and hora shows the short life it gives.

## What the book leaves out {#omitted}

### Ashtakavarga formulas {#ashtakavarga}

There are formulas that estimate longevity from the [ashtakavarga](/en/kb/ashtakavarga). The book says they do not always work and leaves them out; hora does too.

### Shoola dasa {#shoola-dasa}

The book times death with one dasa only, Shoola dasa, in chapters 22 and 23. There the category matters: by whether the life is short, middle or long, one of the three [Trishoola rasis](/en/kb/marakas-and-rudra#trishoola) brings death in its dasa. This is a pointer to those chapters, not a method; hora does not compute Shoola dasa.

## Where hora differs from the book {#differences}

hora was checked against every chart where the book works these methods out (ADR 0017). A few of the book's answers do not come out:

- **Chart 40:** for the first pair the book takes Ketu as the 8th lord, from Aries by Table 32 Scorpio, and reads short life (pp. 276–277). Chapter 15's rule for the stronger lord gives Mars, so hora reads long life for that pair and for the result.
- **Chart 43:** hora's HL is at 0°22′ of Leo, a fixed rasi, where the book reads a movable one, so hora gets middle life where the book gets long (p. 288). The hora lagna depends on the moment of sunrise, and the book's printed HLs fit no sunrise hora can reproduce.
- **Chart 39** (the same birth as chart 14): the book places the death in the middle life range of Shoola dasa (p. 283), which implies middle life; hora's three pairs give short life.
- **Exercise 23:** the eighth lord method's short life is the book's own result, which it says failed.

The marakas and Rudra page lists where hora reads Rudra differently from one worked example.

## On the chart page {#chart-page}

The *Longevity* topic shows, for the rasi chart only, a table of the three pairs, each with its two rasis, whether each is movable, fixed or dual, and its category; then the combined result with its range of years, the rule that combined the pairs, and the paramaayush. Without a Vedic day it says why the third pair is missing. The eighth lord method follows: the reference rasi, the 8th lord, the house he is in from it, and the category. One sentence beside them repeats the book's caution and links here. Everything counts from the lagna, whatever the *Count bhavas from* field says.
