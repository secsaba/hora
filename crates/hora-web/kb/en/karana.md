# Karana

A karana is half of a [tithi](/en/kb/tithi) (lunar day). Every tithi is cut into two karanas, so a [lunar month](/en/kb/lunar-month) of thirty tithis holds sixty of them. The karana at a birth is the last of the five limbs of the panchaanga, hora's almanac of the moment.

## Six degrees at a time

A tithi lasts while the Moon gains 12° on the Sun, measured in the nirayana (sidereal) zodiac; a karana lasts while it gains 6°. The first karana of the month runs from the new moon, when the Moon is 0° ahead, until it is 6° ahead, the second from 6° to 12°, and so on: sixty halves of 6° around the circle. Like a tithi, a karana has no fixed length on the clock. It is usually a little under half a day, and can start and end at any hour.

## Eleven names for sixty halves

There are only eleven karanas, so most names come back many times a month. They are of two kinds.

- **Seven that repeat**: Bava, Balava, Kaulava, Taitula, Garija, Vanija and Vishti. They follow each other in this order, round after round, through the 2nd half of the month to the 57th: 56 halves, eight full rounds of seven.
- **Four that are fixed**: Sakuna, Chatushpada, Naga and Kimstughna. Each falls on the same half in every month. The last three halves of the month are Sakuna, the second half of Krishna Chaturdasi, then Chatushpada and Naga, the two halves of Amavasya, the new moon. Kimstughna comes straight after the new moon, as the first half of Sukla Pratipat, and so opens the month.

The fixed four thus sit together around the new moon, and the seven repeating karanas fill the rest of the month between them.

## A worked example

On 1 January 2000 at 12:00 UT, at Greenwich, the [tithi](/en/kb/tithi) page finds the Moon 302°56′57″ ahead of the Sun ([see the chart](/en/chart?date=2000-01-01&time=12%3A00&calendar=gregorian&utc=stated&offset=%2B00%3A00&latitude=51.48&longitude=0&altitude=&ayanamsa=lahiri&node=mean&sunrise=upper-limb-apparent&reference=lagna&style=south)).

1. 302°56′57″ divided by 6° is 50, with 2°56′57″ left over: fifty halves are complete, so the 51st is running, and the Moon has 3°03′03″ still to gain before it ends.
2. The 51st half is the first half of the 26th tithi, Krishna Ekadasi.
3. The first half of the month is Kimstughna, so the 51st is the 50th of the repeating halves. Seven rounds of seven make 49, so it opens the eighth round: the karana is Bava. The table's row for Krishna Ekadasi says the same.
