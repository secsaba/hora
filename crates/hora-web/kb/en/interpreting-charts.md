# Interpreting charts

Reading a chart means putting together everything the earlier pages teach, and for that, the book says, nothing replaces experience (§13.1). It points readers to the classics, and to B.V. Raman's *How to Judge a Horoscope*, for the standard results of each graha in each bhava and each bhava's lord in each bhava. Chapter 13 itself gives a short method for choosing where in a chart to look, a way to read relatives from one's own chart, and worked examples, among them the [Tapaswi yoga](/en/kb/other-popular-yogas#tapaswi).

## Where to look {#guidelines}

Each question about a life has to be put to the right place in the chart. The book lists the choices in order (§13.4.1).

### The varga chakra {#varga}

Each [varga chakra](/en/kb/vargas) shows one area of life, so the first choice is the chart that belongs to the matter: the [D-9](/en/kb/vargas#d-9) for marriage, the [D-10](/en/kb/vargas#d-10) for career and standing in society, the [D-16](/en/kb/vargas#d-16) for the comfort of vehicles, the [D-20](/en/kb/vargas#d-20) for religious life, the [D-24](/en/kb/vargas#d-24) for learning, the [D-30](/en/kb/vargas#d-30) for the bent of a criminal mind. The book lets culture move this choice: where marriage is lived as a practical arrangement rather than a sacred bond, it would look at the rasi chart before the D-9.

### The bhava {#house}

Within that chart, choose the bhava that carries the matter. A bhava stands for many things, and the varga decides which of them it speaks of: the 9th, for instance, shows the father in the D-12 but religion in the D-20, as on the [bhava significations](/en/kb/bhava-significations) page.

### The reference point {#reference}

Then choose what to count from. The lagna is the true self: what a person is and can do. The [arudha lagna](/en/kb/arudha-padas#arudha-lagna) is the self as others see it, so a matter of reputation, such as how one's work is regarded, is better counted from AL. Where a [karaka](/en/kb/karakas) of the matter is stronger, it can serve as the reference point instead of the lagna.

### Bhava or arudha {#arudha}

Some matters are better seen in a [bhava arudha](/en/kb/arudha-padas#bhava-arudha) than in the bhava itself. A bhava is the thing as it is; its arudha is the thing as it appears, its *maya*. Titles, prizes and published works are how the world sees one's gifts, so they belong to the arudha of the bhava that holds those gifts.

### The influences {#influences}

With the place chosen, judge what acts on it: the grahas that aspect it by [rasi drishti](/en/kb/aspects-and-argalas#rasi-drishti) and [graha drishti](/en/kb/aspects-and-argalas#graha-drishti), its [argalas](/en/kb/aspects-and-argalas#argala), and what each of them means for the matter. Then treat the chosen place as a lagna of its own and see where the grahas fall from it, using the [bhava categories](/en/kb/bhava-categories): a [kendra](/en/kb/bhava-categories#kendra), [trikona](/en/kb/bhava-categories#trikona) or [upachaya](/en/kb/bhava-categories#upachaya) from it works for the matter, a [dusthana](/en/kb/bhava-categories#dusthana) against it. Its [baadhaka](/en/kb/baadhaka-and-yogada#baadhaka-sthaana) can trouble it.

### Standard results {#standard-results}

Last comes the stock of classical readings for each graha and each bhava lord in each bhava, which the book expects a reader to have to hand. None of this stands apart from how strong each graha is, how many [ashtakavarga](/en/kb/ashtakavarga) points a bhava has, and which [yogas](/en/kb/yogas) the chart holds.

## Relatives {#relatives}

One's own chart also shows the fortunes of one's family (§13.4.2). Each relative has a varga chakra:

- parents, grandparents, uncles and aunts: the [D-12](/en/kb/vargas#d-12);
- children, their spouses and grandchildren: the [D-7](/en/kb/vargas#d-7);
- brothers and sisters and their spouses: the [D-3](/en/kb/vargas#d-3);
- the spouse and the spouse's family: the [D-9](/en/kb/vargas#d-9).

In that chart, find the bhava that shows the relative, and take the rasi holding that bhava's lord as the relative's lagna. The arudha pada of that bhava can be read for the relative as well. The 9th shows the father and the 4th the mother, both in the D-12; the 7th shows the spouse, in the D-9.

### Siblings and children {#siblings-and-children}

In the D-3 the 3rd shows the first younger sibling and the 11th the first elder one. The 3rd from the 3rd, the 5th, is the younger sibling's younger sibling, so the second younger; the 7th is the third younger. In the same way the 11th from the 11th, the 9th, is the second elder sibling, and the 7th the third elder. In the D-7 the 5th shows the first child, and each child after is the 3rd from the one before, a younger sibling: the 7th the second, the 9th the third.

For siblings and children the count depends on the lagna of that chart. From an odd lagna, count forward as usual. From an even lagna, count backward. With a D-7 lagna in Taurus, an even rasi, the 5th counted backward from Taurus is Capricorn, so the first child's lagna is the rasi holding Saturn.

Each line steps two rasis at a time, so it stays among rasis of the lagna's own kind, odd or even, and would land on the lagna again at the sixth sibling or the fifth child. The book's NOTE says a long line does not wrap round like that but crosses into rasis of the other kind; it does not show where the crossing lands. hora offers three of each, so the NOTE never comes into play.

### Grandparents, uncles and aunts {#other-relatives}

The book puts these in the D-12 with the parents but gives them no bhava. The bhava of a bhava gives a start: the 9th from the 9th, the 5th, can show the father's father, as on the [bhava significations](/en/kb/bhava-significations) page. hora does not compute them.

### On the chart page {#relatives-chart-page}

The *Count bhavas from* field offers the father, the mother, the spouse, the first three younger and elder siblings and the first three children. Each takes the rasi of its bhava's [stronger lord](/en/kb/arudha-padas#stronger-lord), counting from the lagna of the chart being read, in every chart: its label names the varga the book reads it in, but the choice applies to the rasi chart and to whichever varga chakra is shown. Counting from a relative's lagna changes the bhavas the page shows, not its yogas or ashtakavarga, which always count from the lagna (ADR 0014). hora does not compute a relative's arudha pada.

The book's own case is chart 14, Rajiv Gandhi, whose D-3 lagna is in Sagittarius, an odd rasi (p. 176). The 3rd is Aquarius and its lord Saturn is in Libra, so Libra is the lagna of his younger brother, Sanjay Gandhi. The book then sets Rajiv Gandhi's D-3, read from Libra, beside Sanjay Gandhi's own rasi chart and finds the same placements echoed in both, as it expects between relatives' charts.

## Tapaswi yoga {#tapaswi}

Tapas is sustained, austere effort, and a tapaswi is one devoted to it, whatever the field. The yoga joins three grahas whose meanings add up to such a life: Saturn's toil, Ketu's detachment and Venus's ardour. Two in one rasi with the third aspecting them make the Tapaswi yoga (§13.5). Two things strengthen it: the [atma karaka](/en/kb/karakas#atma-karaka) joining in, which ties the effort to the soul's past, and one of the three standing in the 8th bhava. Its rule as hora checks it, and its results, are on the [other popular yogas](/en/kb/other-popular-yogas#tapaswi) page.

The book's example is chart 13 (pp. 172–175), where Saturn and Ketu are in Virgo and an exalted Venus aspects them from Pisces, the 8th bhava; the atma karaka, by hora's count Saturn, is among them.

## Parivraaja yogas {#parivraaja}

Parivraaja yogas are combinations that give renunciation of the material world. The book names them only in a footnote and gives no rule, so hora does not compute them. Its reading of chart 13 rests on three signs: the Moon, the mind, debilitated in the arudha lagna; Saturn aspecting her; and Saturn joined by Ketu. Together the book reads them as renunciation. Since the chart also holds a strong Raaja yoga, it reads the person as a renunciate who still held real public authority.
