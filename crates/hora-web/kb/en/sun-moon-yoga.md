# Sun-Moon yoga

A Sun-Moon yoga is a measure of the moment taken from the Sun and the Moon together. Where a [tithi](/en/kb/tithi) (lunar day) looks at how far apart the two are, the Sun-Moon yoga adds their longitudes. There are 27 of them, from Vishkambha to Vaidhriti, and the one running at a birth is one of the five limbs of the panchaanga, hora's almanac of the moment.

## Adding the Sun and the Moon

Take the Sun's longitude and the Moon's, both in the nirayana (sidereal) zodiac, and add them. The sum can reach almost 720°; when it passes 360°, take 360° away, so it goes round the circle once only. Then cut the circle into 27 equal parts of 13°20′, the same length as a [nakshatra](/en/kb/nakshatra) (lunar mansion): a sum from 0° to 13°20′ is the first Sun-Moon yoga, Vishkambha, one from 13°20′ to 26°40′ the second, and so on, up to Vaidhriti, the 27th, which ends at 360°.

Both bodies move forward, so the sum always grows, at the Sun's speed plus the Moon's, a little over 14° a day. A Sun-Moon yoga therefore usually lasts a little less than a day, and, like a tithi, can start and end at any hour.

## Not a yoga of grahas

Later in the book, in chapter 11, the word yoga means something else: a combination of [grahas](/en/kb/graha) (planets) in a chart, such as two of them in the same house, that is read for a particular result. Those yogas are patterns of placement and many can be present in one chart at once. The Sun-Moon yoga is just a number from the sum of two longitudes, and exactly one of them runs at any moment. To keep the two apart, hora always calls this one the Sun-Moon yoga.

## A worked example

On 1 January 2000 at 12:00 UT, at Greenwich, hora places the Sun at 16°31′17″ in Sagittarius, a longitude of 256°31′17″, and the Moon at 19°28′14″ in Libra, a longitude of 199°28′14″ ([see the chart](/en/chart?date=2000-01-01&time=12%3A00&calendar=gregorian&utc=stated&offset=%2B00%3A00&latitude=51.48&longitude=0&altitude=&ayanamsa=lahiri&node=mean&sunrise=upper-limb-apparent&reference=lagna&style=south); the [rasi](/en/kb/rasi) page shows how a position in a rasi becomes a longitude).

1. The Sun plus the Moon is 256°31′17″ + 199°28′14″ = 455°59′31″. That is more than 360°, so take 360° away: 95°59′31″.
2. Seven parts of 13°20′ make 93°20′, which leaves 2°39′31″: seven Sun-Moon yogas are complete, so the eighth is running, and the sum has 10°40′29″ still to grow before it ends.
3. In the table, number 8 is Dhriti.
