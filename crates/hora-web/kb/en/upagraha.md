# Upagraha

Besides the nine [grahas](/en/kb/graha) (planets) and the lagna (ascendant), a chart holds eleven upagrahas (sub-planets). Despite the name, no body in the sky answers to them: each is a point of the zodiac worked out by a rule handed down from the sage Parasara. Once found, an upagraha sits in a [rasi](/en/kb/rasi) (sign), a [bhava](/en/kb/bhava) (house) and a [nakshatra](/en/kb/nakshatra) (constellation) like any graha. The eleven fall into two groups by how they are found: five from the Sun's longitude, six from the lagna at a moment of the birth's day or night.

## Sun-based upagrahas {#sun-based}

Dhuma, Vyatipaata, Parivesha, Indrachaapa and Upaketu depend on nothing but the Sun's longitude, and each follows from the one before. Dhuma stands 133°20′ ahead of the Sun. Vyatipaata is Dhuma reflected: 360° less Dhuma's longitude. Parivesha is opposite Vyatipaata. Indrachaapa is Parivesha reflected, 360° less its longitude. Upaketu stands 16°40′ ahead of Indrachaapa, which always brings it to 30° behind the Sun. A sum past 360° or a difference below 0° is brought back into the circle by a full turn.

Followed through, three of them come out as the Sun's longitude plus or minus a fixed amount, and the two reflected ones as a fixed amount less the Sun's longitude. So Dhuma and Indrachaapa are always opposite each other, and so are Vyatipaata and Parivesha; and the two reflected ones run backwards through the zodiac while the Sun moves forwards.

All five are very malefic. A bhava that holds one of them is spoiled by it, in the rasi chart (rasi chakra) and in the [varga chakras](/en/kb/varga-chakra) (divisional charts) alike.

## Time-based upagrahas {#time-based}

Kaala, Mrityu, Arthaprahaara, Yamaghantaka, Gulika and Maandi are each the lagna rising at one moment near the birth. To find that moment, take the birth's [Vedic day](/en/kb/vaara-and-hora), from sunrise to the next sunrise, and split it into its day, from sunrise to sunset, and its night, from sunset to the next sunrise; a birth at the very moment of sunset belongs to the night. The half that holds the birth is cut into eight equal parts. Day and night are rarely the same length, so a day's parts and a night's parts differ too.

Seven of the eight parts are ruled by the seven planets, one each, in the order of the weekdays: Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn. The part after Saturn's has no lord, and then the Sun's comes round again. By day, the first part belongs to the lord of the vaara (weekday). By night, the count starts four planets further on in that order: on a Sunday night, the first part is Jupiter's.

Each of the six rises at the start or the middle of its planet's part:

- Kaala at the middle of the Sun's part;
- Mrityu at the middle of Mars's part;
- Arthaprahaara at the middle of Mercury's part;
- Yamaghantaka at the middle of Jupiter's part;
- Gulika at the start of Saturn's part;
- Maandi at the middle of Saturn's part.

The Moon's part, Venus's part and the lord-less part give none. Chapter 4 of the book prints Gulika and Maandi the other way round, but the book's own example in chapter 36, which finds Gulika within a degree of the lagna, works only with Gulika at the start of the part, as the author's software places it; hora follows that example and the software.

## A worked example

Take hora's chart for 1 January 2000 at 06:00 UT, at Greenwich ([see the chart](/en/chart?date=2000-01-01&time=06%3A00&calendar=gregorian&utc=stated&offset=%2B00%3A00&latitude=51.48&longitude=0&altitude=&ayanamsa=lahiri&node=mean&sunrise=upper-limb-apparent&reference=lagna&style=south)). Its lagna is in Scorpio.

1. The Sun is at 16°15′59″ Sagittarius, a longitude of 256°15′59″. Adding 133°20′ gives 389°35′59″, a turn past the circle: Dhuma is at 29°35′59″ Aries, and Indrachaapa, opposite, at 29°35′59″ Libra.
2. Vyatipaata is 360° less Dhuma, 330°24′: 0°24′ Pisces. Parivesha, opposite, is at 0°24′ Virgo.
3. Upaketu, 30° behind the Sun, is at 16°15′59″ Scorpio, in the lagna's own rasi.
4. Sunrise in Greenwich that morning came at 08:05:46, so the birth still belongs to the Vedic day that began on Friday, 31 December, and to its night, which started at sunset, 15:59:58. That night is just over 16 hours long, and each of its parts just over 2 hours. A Friday night's parts run Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, the lord-less part, Sun, Moon.
5. Saturn's is the 5th part, from about 00:03 to 02:04. The lagna rising at its start is at 13°41′ Virgo: that is Gulika. At its middle, about 01:03, it is at 24°20′ Virgo: Maandi.
6. The birth falls in the 7th part, the Sun's, from about 04:04 to 06:05. Kaala, at its middle, is at 6°55′ Scorpio, beside Upaketu in the lagna's rasi.

## In the chart

The chart page lists the upagrahas in a table of their own, under the grahas, and leaves them out of the drawing. Each row gives the rasi, the bhava counted from the chosen reference point, the position, the nakshatra and pada (quarter), the nakshatra's Vimsottari lord and the rasi's lord. As for the lagna, the table shows no dignity and only the rasi's lord, without a relationship. A chart without a Vedic day lists only the five Sun-based upagrahas. hora places the upagrahas in the rasi chart only; in the varga chakras they wait for chapter 6.
