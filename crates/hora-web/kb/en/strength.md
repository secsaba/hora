# Strength

Reading a chart keeps asking how strong something is. Which of two grahas acts as a rasi's lord? Which of two rasis starts a dasa? When several [grahas](/en/kb/graha) (planets) pull a bhava different ways, whose pull wins? Chapter 15 answers that there is no single strength: there are several, each measuring something different, and each belongs to its own questions (§15.1–15.2).

## Why more than one {#why}

Think of the different things one might want to know about a graha:

- **Who leads.** When several grahas take part in one yoga, or all influence one bhava, the strongest acts for the group and gives the results. That is shadbala.
- **Who works well with the rest.** A graha that is capable on its own still delivers little when it is at odds with the other forces in the chart. That is ashtakavarga bala, and it can differ from one varga chakra to another, so a graha may give its results well in the area of life of one varga chakra and poorly in another's.
- **What state it is in.** Capacity and harmony are not enough if the graha is, so to speak, asleep or miserable. Those are the [avasthas](/en/kb/avasthas).
- **What part it plays in the whole life.** Not its results in one area, but how large a role it has overall. That is vimsopaka bala.
- **Who goes first, in small matters.** Which of two lords or two rasis starts a dasa or acts in an arudha pada is settled by a short list of simple rules, unlike any of the above.

The book's point is that these measures are not interchangeable. Using one where another belongs only confuses the reading; one should know what each means and pick the one the question needs.

## Shadbala {#shadbala}

Shadbala, "six-fold strength", adds up six sources of a graha's strength: its placement, the time, its direction, the aspects on it, its motion and its own nature (§15.3). Of the grahas that influence one bhava or join in one yoga, the one with the highest shadbala is the likeliest to give the results, like the leader of a group acting on its behalf.

## Ashtakavarga bala {#ashtakavarga-bala}

Ashtakavarga bala shows how far the other grahas support or oppose a graha (§15.3). It does not say whether the graha can lead a group, as shadbala does, but how well it gives its own results in harmony with the forces around it. The book does not say how to compute it from the [ashtakavarga](/en/kb/ashtakavarga) tables of chapter 12.

## Vimsopaka bala {#vimsopaka-bala}

Vimsopaka bala, "twenty-point strength", is a graha's overall strength across the varga chakras: how important a role it plays in the native's life as a whole, rather than in one area of it (§15.2). It is not the [amsabala](/en/kb/amsabala) count of chapter 6, which names the dignities a graha holds in a group of varga chakras.

## What hora does not compute {#not-computed}

hora computes none of the three balas (ADR 0018). The book explains what they are for but not how to compute them: it calls shadbala's computation beyond its scope, points to other works, and notes that the programs that compute it differ slightly in their definitions. It works no values that a computation could be checked against, and hora's calculations follow the book and are checked against its worked examples.

hora does show the [ashtakavarga](/en/kb/ashtakavarga) tables, the [amsabala](/en/kb/amsabala) count and the avasthas, each on its own page and topic.

## The simple rules {#simple-rules}

For the small decisions, which of two co-lords acts for Scorpio or Aquarius and which of two rasis is the stronger, the book gives two short lists of rules tried in order (§15.5). They are on the arudha padas page, which needs them first: the [stronger lord](/en/kb/arudha-padas#stronger-lord) and the [stronger rasi](/en/kb/arudha-padas#stronger-rasi). hora uses the stronger lord in the arudha padas and in the pairs of [longevity](/en/kb/longevity#three-pairs), and the same rules choose the stronger of two grahas for the [sthira karakas](/en/kb/karakas#father-and-mother) of the father and the mother.

Two parts of §15.5 wait for the dasas, which hora does not compute yet:

- **Dasa length.** When the first four rules tie, a co-lord that gives its rasi the longer dasa is the stronger for that dasa. Everywhere hora compares two grahas, it uses the rule for arudha padas instead: the more advanced wins.
- **Other dasas weigh rasis differently.** The book warns that its rules are general and meant for Narayana dasa and other phalita dasas, where the aspects of Mercury, Jupiter and the rasi's lord count. In ayur dasas, which show longevity, the aspects of the Sun and the Moon matter, every other graha's aspect counts the same, and the lord's aspect counts for nothing. In dasas built on the atma karaka, the rasi holding the [atma karaka](/en/kb/karakas#atma-karaka) beats every other, and after the aspects one looks at where the rasi's lord stands from it: a lord in a kendra from it is stronger than one in a panaphara. hora applies only the general rules.

## In the chart {#in-the-chart}

The chart page's *Strength* topic shows the avasthas, the stronger lord of Scorpio and Aquarius with the rule that decided it, and the sthira karakas, the father's and the mother's with their deciding rule.
