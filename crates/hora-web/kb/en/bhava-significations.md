# What the bhavas show

A [bhava](/en/kb/bhava) (house) is a rasi counted from a reference point. Each of the twelve stands for some areas of life, and what it shows in a given chart depends on two more things: the reference point it is counted from, and the [varga chakra](/en/kb/varga-chakra) (divisional chart) it is counted in. This page goes through all three.

## The twelve bhavas

Each bhava covers many matters. The lines below give the thread that runs through each one's matters, with the main ones named; the book lists more for each, and the classics more still. Taken in order, the bhavas also map the body from the head, at the 1st, down to the feet, at the 12th.

### 1st bhava {#bhava-1}

The person: the body, its looks and vigour, character and intelligence, and the circumstances of birth. Success and renown that come from who one is belong here too.

### 2nd bhava {#bhava-2}

What one holds and takes in: money and possessions, the family one lives among, food, the face and mouth, and so speech.

### 3rd bhava {#bhava-3}

One's own effort: courage and willpower, initiative and creative work, communicating, short journeys, and the younger brothers and sisters and close companions who stand at one's side.

### 4th bhava {#bhava-4}

The home ground: the mother, the house and land, vehicles and other property, the homeland, schooling, and the inner side of home life, contentment and peace of mind.

### 5th bhava {#bhava-5}

What one brings forth: children, intelligence and learning, judgement, devotion and prayer, the merit earned in earlier lives, love and feeling, speculation, and the authority that comes with them.

### 6th bhava {#bhava-6}

Struggle: enemies and rivals, illness, injuries and accidents, worry, and work done in service of others, including servants and those one serves.

### 7th bhava {#bhava-7}

Others as equals: the spouse and married life, desire, partners in business, trade, and journeys far from home.

### 8th bhava {#bhava-8}

What lies under the surface or strikes without warning: how long one lives, illness that lingers, what one owes, shame, the occult and everything kept secret, and money that arrives unearned, by bequest or by luck.

### 9th bhava {#bhava-9}

Guidance and grace: the father, teachers and those above one, fortune, religion and dharma (duty), higher learning, travel abroad, compassion and generosity.

### 10th bhava {#bhava-10}

Action in the world: the work one is known for, one's karma (deeds), how one carries oneself in public, and the recognition and dignity that follow from it.

### 11th bhava {#bhava-11}

Gains: what one earns and the hopes that come true, the circle of friends, and older brothers and sisters.

### 12th bhava {#bhava-12}

Letting go: money spent and things lost, the night and its rest and pleasures, being shut away from the world, enemies who work out of sight, a life lived far from home, charity, meditation and, at the end of the road, moksha (liberation).

## Bhavas counted from bhavas

Any bhava can itself be a reference point, and the meanings combine. To find the nth bhava from a bhava, count on n − 1 rasis from it: the 7th from the 5th is the 11th (5 + 7 − 1), and past the 12th, take away 12. The 5th shows children and the 7th a partner, so the 11th can show a child's spouse. In the same way the 9th from the 9th, the 5th bhava, can show the father's father.

## Reading a bhava in a varga chakra

Each bhava lists many matters; the varga chakra decides which of them a question is about. Every [varga chakra](/en/kb/vargas) shows one area of life, and in it a bhava takes the meaning that belongs to that area. The 9th bhava, for instance, shows the father in the dwadasamsa (D-12), the chart of parents; religion and spiritual practice in the vimsamsa (D-20); and teachers and higher studies in the chaturvimsamsa (D-24), the chart of learning. The bhavas of a varga chakra are counted in that chart, from the reference point's rasi there.

## The reference points

Each reference point looks at the chart from its own angle, and colours every bhava counted from it. The 10th bhava, the career, shows one thing from the lagna, another from the Moon and another from the hora lagna. The most used reference points are these.

### Lagna {#lagna}

The lagna (ascendant) stands for the true self: who one is, what one means to do, what one knows and how firmly one holds to it. It is the default: a bhava named without a reference point is counted from the lagna. It is the wrong reference for how others see one, such as rank or reputation.

### Chandra lagna {#chandra-lagna}

Chandra lagna (Moon lagna) is the Moon taken as the reference point. The Moon is the mind, so bhavas counted from it show how one experiences a matter: how happy one is, how ambitious, how one feels about one's work. Two people whose 4th from the lagna looks alike can feel very differently at home; the 4th from the Moon shows how settled each one is.

### Ravi lagna {#ravi-lagna}

Ravi lagna (Sun lagna) is the Sun taken as the reference point. The Sun is the soul, so bhavas counted from it show matters as the soul meets them. It is also the reference for physical vitality.

### Paaka lagna {#paaka-lagna}

Paaka lagna is the rasi that holds the lagna's lord, taken as the reference point. Where the lagna is the self as an idea, its lord is the person who physically lives that self, so bhavas counted from paaka lagna show matters as they touch one's bodily existence: health, energy, the life one physically leads. The book uses it in reading the rasi chart and the varga chakras, the [dasas](/en/kb/dasa) (periods) and the transits (where the grahas are on a later day).

In a varga chakra, paaka lagna is found inside that varga chakra: take the rasi the lagna falls in there, and its lord, and see which rasi the lord occupies there. It is not the rasi chart's lagna lord carried over.

A Scorpio or Aquarius lagna has two lords: Mars and Ketu for Scorpio, Saturn and Rahu for Aquarius. The book chooses between them, by strength, only in chapter 15; until then hora takes Mars or Saturn, the lord the chart page's Rasi lord column shows.

### Ghati lagna {#ghati-lagna}

The [ghati lagna](/en/kb/special-lagna#ghati-lagna) (GL) shows the self through power: authority, rank and fame. It is the reference for a promotion at work or for a politician's power.

### Hora lagna {#hora-lagna}

The [hora lagna](/en/kb/special-lagna#hora-lagna) (HL) shows the self through wealth, and is the reference for questions of money.

### Graha lagna {#graha-lagna}

A graha lagna (planetary reference) is a graha taken as the reference point for the bhavas it naturally signifies. To judge a bhava, look at it counted from the lagna and also counted from its graha, and weigh the two; if the graha is the stronger of the two, its count weighs more. The grahas and the bhavas they signify, bhava by bhava:

- the 1st and 2nd: the Moon;
- the 3rd: Mars;
- the 4th: the Moon;
- the 5th: Jupiter;
- the 6th: Mercury;
- the 7th: Venus;
- the 8th: Saturn;
- the 9th: the Sun and the Moon;
- the 10th: the Sun;
- the 11th: the Sun and the Moon;
- the 12th: Saturn.

So illness is judged from the 6th counted from the lagna and the 6th counted from Mercury; income from the 11th from the lagna and the 11th from the Sun or the Moon. Rahu and Ketu signify no bhava here. A graha that signifies a particular matter, not a whole bhava, can be a reference point in the same way, in the varga chakra of that matter; the book lists those graha significators in chapter 8.

### Coming later

Two more reference points come with later chapters: karakamsa, the rasi of the atma karaka (the graha that stands for the soul) in the navamsa, with chapter 8, and arudha lagna, which shows how the world sees one and one's status, with chapter 9.

## A worked example

Take hora's chart for 1 January 2000 at 12:00 UT, at Greenwich, the chart of the bhava page's example ([see the chart](/en/chart?date=2000-01-01&time=12%3A00&calendar=gregorian&utc=stated&offset=%2B00%3A00&latitude=51.48&longitude=0&altitude=&ayanamsa=lahiri&node=mean&sunrise=upper-limb-apparent&reference=paaka-lagna&style=south)). Its lagna is in Aries, whose lord is Mars, and Mars is in Aquarius: Aquarius is the paaka lagna.

1. Counted from the paaka lagna, Aquarius is the 1st bhava and Mars is in it. The lagna, Jupiter and Saturn, in Aries, are in the 3rd; the Moon, in Libra, in the 9th; Venus, in Scorpio, in the 10th; the Sun and Mercury, in Sagittarius, in the 11th.
2. The 10th bhava is a different rasi from each reference point. From the lagna it is Capricorn, which holds Ketu; from the Moon, in Libra, it is Cancer, which holds Rahu; from the paaka lagna it is Scorpio, which holds Venus. Each answers its own question about the career: what one sets out to do, how one feels about it, and what one does with one's own hands and energy.
3. For marriage, the 7th from the lagna is Libra, holding the Moon; Venus, the graha lagna of the 7th, is in Scorpio, and the 7th from it is Taurus, which is empty.
4. In the chaturvimsamsa (D-24) the paaka lagna is found afresh ([see the chart](/en/chart?date=2000-01-01&time=12%3A00&calendar=gregorian&utc=stated&offset=%2B00%3A00&latitude=51.48&longitude=0&altitude=&ayanamsa=lahiri&node=mean&sunrise=upper-limb-apparent&reference=paaka-lagna&style=north&varga=d24)). There the lagna is in Leo, whose lord is the Sun, and the Sun is in Virgo, so Virgo is the D-24 paaka lagna, with Jupiter and Saturn beside the Sun. Mars, in Scorpio in D-24, plays no part.

## In the chart

The *Count bhavas from* field on the home page sets the reference point for the chart page: the lagna, any of the nine grahas, the hora, ghati or sree lagna, or paaka lagna. The Bhava columns and, in the North Indian style, the drawings follow it, the varga chakra drawing counting from the reference point's rasi in that varga.
